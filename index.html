Abstract

During launch and early orbit operations, ground stations must acquire spacecraft signals before reliable telemetry and commanding can begin. An initial orbit estimate predicts a pass, but pointing uncertainty can span many antenna beamwidths. We introduce Bayesian Search Model Predictive Control (BS-MPC), which updates an orbit belief after unsuccessful observations and chooses antenna pointings subject to motion, settling, and dwell constraints. A feasible continuation accounts for search opportunities beyond the controller’s short lookahead. In a synthetic first-contact benchmark spanning 96 deployments, BS-MPC increases acquisition probability from 83.3% for a uniform tube sweep to 90.2%, while reducing restricted mean acquisition time from 89.8 to 78.3 seconds. LEOPT provides the reusable search solvers and application code for studying these decisions.

Bayesian search MPC

The controller maintains weighted orbit hypotheses. After a missed detection, hypotheses that predicted a signal lose weight. The next pointing depends on this updated belief and on the directions the antenna can reach within its rate, acceleration, settling, and dwell limits.

BS-MPC starts with a feasible tube sweep through the contact. A beam search proposes short pointing sequences that can rejoin the remaining sweep. A proposal replaces the current plan only if it improves acquisition probability over the full remaining contact. This continuation preserves coverage beyond the four-dwell lookahead.

Belief update after a miss

wi+= wi[1−pi(a)] ∑jwj[1−pj(a)]

For a binary detection model with no false confirmations, pi(a) is the detection probability for hypothesis i at pointing a.

Simulation results

The first-contact benchmark includes 2,773 reserved search attempts across 96 deployments. All controllers receive the same reservations, with matched orbital uncertainty, a constrained antenna mount, and a 192-second search window.

The planned evaluation uses Kayhan Space LEOP records to test acquisition searches with mission-derived orbit uncertainty and receiver behavior. Controllers use only information available before each contact; calibration and evaluation use separate deployments.

Explore the solver

LEOPT contains the search solvers and acquisition application under the MIT license. The browser console lets you compare BS-MPC, MPC without continuation, Bayesian and frozen-prior greedy search, prior-ranked sweep, tube sweep, and sky raster on a sample mission.
